Today I passed CySA+. That makes three certifications — CC, SSCP, and CySA+ — in a forty-four-day window. I'm writing this with the post-exam fog still in place, partly to mark the moment and partly to lock in some honest notes before the lessons get romanticized.

## The actual sequence

- **CC** (ISC2) — the easiest of the three, and the right one to start with. It's a confidence builder more than a credential, but it gets you in the ISC2 ecosystem.
- **SSCP** (ISC2) — the meaningful jump. The domains are deeper, and the framing is operational. This is the one that earned its place.
- **CySA+** (CompTIA) — the closer. Heavily scenario-based; the questions test whether you can read an artifact and reason about it, not just recall a definition.

## What worked

A few things, in order of how much they mattered:

1. **Binge a domain in one sitting, then drill.** Splitting study across short daily sessions never worked for me — I lost the thread between sessions. Long single-domain blocks let me build the mental model once and then prove it to myself with question banks.
2. **Multiple question sources.** ISC2's official LearnZapp, PocketPrep, and the OSG practice booklets each test in slightly different shapes. Drilling all three caught the gaps any one of them missed.
3. **Physical books for practice tests.** Mostly because the screen-fatigue tax during a long study day is real. The official CompTIA practice booklets in particular are worth their cover price.
4. **Firm exam dates.** Scheduling the exam *before* I felt ready is what made me actually ready. Open-ended study expands to fill the available time; a date on the calendar collapses it.

## What didn't

- **Forcing through fatigue.** I tried this on a couple of bad days and the retention was visibly worse the next morning. I stopped, and the schedule still held.
- **Watching video at 1.0x.** Mike Chapple's LinkedIn Learning courses are excellent at 1.5x — at normal speed they're a sleep aid for me.

## Would I do it again?

For this opening sequence — yes. There was a real benefit to compressing the timeline: each cert's material reinforced the next, and the discipline carried over. Cleared in the same window, the three feel like one continuous push rather than three separate ones.

For *every* cert? No. The next stop is the AWS track, and I'm pacing that one. Cloud platforms reward hands-on time in a way that pure security knowledge doesn't, and trying to compress that is how you end up with paper credentials and shallow skills. I'd rather have the AWS Cloud Practitioner *and* the muscle memory.

The next post will be from the other side of that exam. See you then.
